## Adoption assessment

The guidance has value as an optional, local operating hint for the demonstrated
tool environment. Default adoption of the whole append is not recommended.
The [earlier call chain][earlier-chain] and RSP assessment establish recurring
wrapper and dispatched-command construction failures; this pair adds a
positive observation without establishing that the prompt caused it. Both arms
already completed the task correctly, so improved final success probability
has not been demonstrated.

The most useful part to retain as a candidate is the preference for short
commands and, when needed, a file-editing step followed by script execution.
The candidate actually used that path without observed construction failure or
acceptance regression. This supports its practical feasibility, not a mandate
to create a script for simple commands. The String.raw branch has weaker
adoption evidence: earlier successful calls and bounded byte-preservation
checks support that technique under their stated conditions, but neither arm
used it here. Its contribution to model behavior remains untested.

The append adds 769 bytes of prompt text and asks the model to choose a literal
form or manage a temporary script and retained evidence. These are additional
reading, decision and file-management obligations. Their marginal token and
time costs have not been isolated: the candidate's 12,254 additional input
tokens cannot be attributed entirely to the append. Lower output and shorter
elapsed time likewise do not establish net savings, given different workloads
and cache histories. Guidance about checking effects after failure reinforces
existing authority boundaries; it does not justify another Core/Commit rule.

One fixed-order pair on one task and model is insufficient to recommend this
as a universal default. The technique depends on available editing tools and
the command wrapper; portability and repeated reliability remain unknown.
Adding it only to an evaluation task would change that task's assistance, not
establish an improvement in RSP itself. The full append has not been compared
with a shorter version, so the usefulness of its individual sentences cannot
be inferred from the aggregate result.

A separately authorized, reversible local use could reasonably accept this
limited positive evidence as an operational judgment, without claiming proven
efficiency. That is a possible future decision, not adoption in this record.
For now, keep the candidate isolated and do not change the task baseline,
Core/Commit or scoring. This assessment requires no further syntax corpus,
automatic supplemental run or new judge call; stop with the present evidence
and retain the unresolved boundaries rather than testing until adoption
appears justified.

## Review, preservation and stopping decision

The [independent evidence review][review] returned clean. It independently
checked both 70-file input inventories, normalized settings, image references,
native results, session failures, usage and timing. It reused the retained
image/history integrity evidence rather than rerunning containers or hashing
all history. The main review's [preservation check][preservation] separately
confirmed 47,712 historical/current execution files and 1,461 prior tracked
files unchanged. These inventory counts are not numbers of trials.

Original scores, failures, trajectories, frozen inputs and historical reports
remain intact. All 11 calibration labels remain candidate. Core/Commit, scoring
rules and the main task baseline are unchanged. The review evidence directory
also retains the formal document's subsequent consistency review and checks
when completed; the initial evidence review did not cover an unwritten document.

Stop at this single pair. It supports retaining the isolated candidate for a
separate decision, not automatic adoption, a global rule, repeated reliability,
causal speedup or lower fees. Do not supplement or rerun to strengthen this
result without new authority. No syntax corpus or report exception boundary
was expanded.
