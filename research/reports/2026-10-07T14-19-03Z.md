## Useful investigation leads

- Natural-request routing and task benefit remain separate from explicit Skill invocation. Use the existing runner with relevant positive tasks, neighboring negative cases and realistic mixed projects. Choose repetitions and variety for the actual uncertainty, without fixed quotas or another routing framework.
- Measure coordination and reading overhead alongside task success and stability. Compare equivalent tasks and disclose model, effort, host, case, candidate and run conditions. Shorter documents, fewer tools and lower token counts alone do not establish improvement.
- Repository-wide upstream changes can reveal mechanisms outside selected capture paths. Explore these as leads, including unchanged sources that have become locally relevant. Refresh completion is not proof that every change was read.
- Host cancellation, replay and isolation require evidence from the selected host and real recipient inputs. Historical proposals do not justify a runtime database, universal provider state machine, ambient hooks, automatic delivery or another ownership ledger.
- Removal/rename behavior and stale-owner recovery are useful case classes when a concrete change needs them. They are not demonstrated current defects or an automatic new suite.

The September synthesis treated popularity and file churn as discovery signals, not quality measures. Reject keyword-density rewards and automatic promotion, and verify licensing before transferring assets. Its Skill-Use-Bench conclusion allowed model-only learning rather than copying the corpus; deleting the old report does not remove that restriction or certify other assets for reuse.

## Historical evaluation lessons

These results belong to their original candidates and environments. They do not establish current acceptance or current performance.

- The July 21 managed-controller experiment recommended revision rather than promotion. Its two paired scenarios showed no success/correction benefit and higher aggregate input and elapsed time. Process completion and extra coordination can cost more without improving the outcome; those measurements do not describe today's Core.
- The October 5 contract-path campaign initially stopped incomplete. Its later completion recorded seven passing judgments and one inconclusive Doc judgment. An unrelated personal-auth change had been an overbroad orchestration gate, not evidence of product failure.
- A separate fresh Doc execution and judgment subsequently passed. The original Doc judgment remains inconclusive. The fresh evidence located a 9,411-byte tool return whose host/CLI completion retained only a 4,963-byte suffix. Retaining both representations addressed the runner evidence gap; it did not establish that the external host was repaired or conclusively explain the lost historical return.
- The October 5 reading-path probe completed four executions and four judgments: all mechanical checks passed, while judgments were three passes and one failure. The failed Doc judgment had an unsupported serialized-literal interpretation and an inconsistently applied reader-path criterion. Preserve the failure and these limitations together. One serial pair per condition cannot establish general reliability or efficiency gains. Both Doc samples avoided broad search, so the entry-file difference cannot be credited as its cause.

The October completion, Doc repair and reading-path probe used `AI-HUB/gpt-6.1-sol`, executor medium and judge high, with `codex-cli 0.150.1`. The probe cited local HEAD `c630a4ba0d6c917e9fc51533257c23b480d7526c`. Their current-format private evidence remains under `tests/skills/reports/`, including `rsp-contract-path-resume-20261005.T5DvGG`, `rsp-doc-evidence-20261005.DKkvPI` and `rsp-context-probe-20261005.haCe2z`; a checkout alone does not include it.
