## Reuse current contracts, retain historical evidence

Research models preserve their dated mechanisms, dispositions and verdicts. Even an `accepted` model is not a current operating baseline. Before reuse, reconcile its recommendation with [Distribution](../../.rsp/specs/distribution.md), [Workflow and capability ownership](../../.rsp/specs/workflow.md), and the current internal `author-rsp-skills`, `distill-upstream` and `release-acceptance` guidance. Current verification has two lanes; comparisons and holdouts are risk-selected research designs, and candidate handoff uses the smallest sufficient contract. Historical fixed behavior counts, mandatory holdouts and retired infrastructure do not regain authority through reuse.

- For historical Git inputs, inspect the exact cited report and retained material through the archive recovery guidance. The new tool does not validate old dossiers or infer a researched/accepted baseline from them. Missing historical bytes remain an explicit evidence gap.
- For web snapshots, check the report's date, URLs, retained bytes, hashes, retrieval gaps and reuse limits. A matching hash proves retained identity, not current web freshness. Missing bytes leave retention unverified. New retrieval requires authorization; listing active studies does not validate historical snapshots.
- For either source type, check that cited evidence supports the selected inference and remains applicable to the current task. Neither a model's `complete` status nor intact source identity establishes freshness or present acceptance.
